# Mzansi AI Educator — Source-Locked Curriculum Map

Status: Controlled build baseline
Purpose: Prevent curriculum drift while expanding Mzansi AI Educator from beginner to advanced learning.

## 1. Governing product doctrine

Mzansi AI Educator is South African-first, globally informed, Ubuntu-informed, human-centred, equity-sensitive, mobile-first, data-conscious, multilingual-aware, critical rather than blindly pro-AI, practical rather than technology-showcase driven, and scaffolded from beginner through leadership-level capability.

Core promise: make meaningful AI literacy accessible, locally relevant and practically useful without requiring high digital literacy, expensive technology, continuous connectivity or Western-centric examples.

The product must never assume that underserved learners are less capable. Accessibility changes the route into the learning, not the intellectual destination.

## 2. One pathway, same AI language

Mzansi AI Educator serves learners in and out of formal education through one continuous learning pathway.

Technical AI terminology is retained throughout. Terms such as algorithm, training data, inference, predictive model, machine learning, generative AI, token, hallucination, bias, privacy, human oversight and prompt engineering are not removed because they may be unfamiliar.

Permanent teaching rule:

TERM → PLAIN MEANING → EVERYDAY SOUTH AFRICAN CONNECTION → FORMAL MEANING → PRACTICE → USE THE TERM AGAIN → REFLECTION

The language remains consistent across levels. Depth, independence and complexity increase.

## 3. Cape Town-first language foundation

Initial learner language choices:

- English
- Afrikaans
- isiXhosa

The language layer must support:

1. Interface language
2. Plain-language explanation
3. Curriculum terminology reinforcement
4. Glossary support
5. Assessment support where appropriate

Translation must not create three different curricula. There is one concept model with multiple learner-facing language layers.

## 4. Learning progression

### Level 1 — DISCOVER
Purpose: Understand AI and develop confidence.

Minimum scope:
- what AI is and is not
- everyday AI
- human agency
- algorithms
- data and training data
- models
- prediction and classification
- training versus inference
- generative AI foundations
- privacy awareness
- bias and representation
- algorithmic bias
- responsible use
- where AI may be inappropriate
- Ubuntu-informed reflection on community knowledge and human dignity

Learner standard:
The learner can recognise, explain and discuss core AI concepts in their own words while using the correct technical terminology.

### Level 2 — USE
Purpose: Apply AI responsibly.

Minimum scope:
- prompting and instruction design
- CREO prompting framework: Context, Role, Explicit Instructions, Output Format
- practical tool use
- research support
- productivity
- fact-checking
- hallucination awareness
- source verification
- bias awareness
- privacy and data protection
- human accountability
- responsible use in school, work, business and everyday life

Learner standard:
The learner can use AI tools deliberately, explain why a prompt works or fails, verify outputs and identify risks.

### Level 3 — CREATE
Purpose: Design and build with AI.

Minimum scope:
- problem scoping
- deciding whether AI is justified
- workflow design
- localisation
- multilingual design
- low-data/mobile design
- system thinking
- simple model concepts
- prototyping
- testing and evaluation
- feedback and iteration
- ethics by design
- inclusive design
- human oversight

Learner standard:
The learner can design or prototype an AI-enabled solution and explain who it serves, what data or inputs it needs, how it may fail, who could be harmed and how it should be improved.

### Level 4 — LEAD
Purpose: Govern, guide and deploy AI responsibly.

Minimum scope:
- governance
- strategy
- data stewardship
- data sovereignty
- institutional policy
- accountability
- community participation
- responsible deployment
- risk
- inclusion
- African innovation
- AI safety
- South African regulatory and institutional context where applicable

Learner standard:
The learner can evaluate AI adoption decisions, identify governance responsibilities and reason about public, organisational and community consequences.

Note: DISCOVER → USE → CREATE → LEAD is Mzansi product terminology. It is not presented as official UNESCO terminology.

## 5. Permanent lesson standard

Every substantial lesson should contain, where relevant:

1. Learning goal
2. Correct technical term
3. Plain-language meaning
4. Everyday South African connection
5. Formal explanation
6. Worked example
7. Local context example
8. Interactive or unplugged activity
9. Common misconception or failure mode
10. Bias, ethics, privacy or safety consideration
11. Knowledge check
12. Applied scenario
13. Practical task
14. Reflection
15. Assessment
16. Explanation of incorrect answers
17. Recap
18. Glossary reinforcement
19. Locally persisted progress

A lesson must not be considered educationally complete simply because a learner answered a few multiple-choice questions.

## 6. Assessment doctrine

Assessment must use more than quizzes.

Acceptable evidence can include:
- multiple-choice or short-answer checks
- verbal explanation
- mother-tongue explanation where appropriate
- observation sheets
- scenario reasoning
- identifying failure modes
- paper design
- practical demonstrations
- prototypes
- peer critique
- reflection
- community problem-solving tasks

The app must separate quiz submission from lesson completion. Completion should represent meaningful participation or demonstrated learning, not a button press.

## 7. South African contextualisation rule

South African examples should span more than township poverty narratives.

Use contexts such as:
- WhatsApp and mobile communication
- taxis and transport
- spaza and informal retail
- schools and colleges
- public services
- banking and fraud detection
- agriculture
- artisan trades
- small business
- creative industries
- law
- finance
- engineering
- software
- community organisations
- health and public-interest contexts where safely appropriate

Permanent principle: African relevance without African limitation.

## 8. Bias and data inclusion

Algorithmic bias must be treated as a core AI-literacy topic, not an optional ethics sidebar.

Learners should understand that model behaviour depends on data, design choices, assumptions and deployment context. African languages, accents, communities and institutional realities may be poorly represented in global systems.

Teaching pattern:

EVERYDAY FAILURE → WHY IT MAY HAVE HAPPENED → TRAINING DATA / MODEL / DESIGN TERM → WHO IS AFFECTED → HOW TO TEST → HUMAN OVERSIGHT

## 9. Offline and edge-first rule

No essential learner journey should become useless when connectivity is lost.

The app should retain useful offline states for:
- opening downloaded/core lessons
- reading explanations
- glossary access
- unplugged activities
- saved progress
- previously available assessments where technically safe
- local learner preferences

Every activity that depends on connectivity should have a meaningful low-tech or offline fallback where feasible.

Offline resilience does not require every advanced AI feature to work offline. It requires the primary learning purpose to remain useful.

## 10. Device and access rule

Design for ordinary Android phones, limited data, intermittent connectivity, limited storage, low digital confidence and unreliable electricity.

Avoid unnecessary media dependencies. Prefer text, lightweight illustrations, locally cached assets, paper-capable activities and resilient state handling.

## 11. Curriculum depth rule

Depth increases by level, but terminology remains stable.

Progression:

RECOGNISE → EXPLAIN → APPLY → ANALYSE → CREATE → EVALUATE → LEAD

Level advancement must reflect deeper competence, not simply more lessons completed.

## 12. Dashboard implications

The dashboard must serve the learning architecture rather than display every feature at once.

Priority learner actions:
- Continue Learning
- Explore Level / Curriculum
- Practise
- Glossary / Explain a Term
- Progress
- Offline Status

The dashboard should visibly communicate the current Mzansi level and next meaningful learning action.
